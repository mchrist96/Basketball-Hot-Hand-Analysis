Does the hot hand show up in the box score?

A working ledger of the hot hand analysis so far: what the data is, why the obvious test is broken, how the permutation test fixes it, and what four seasons of play-by-play say about Jokić and 304 other players.

1 · The data930,133 shots, in the order they happened

Every field goal attempt from the official play-by-play feed, ordered by game clock, with the shooter, outcome, distance, shot value, whether it was assisted, the score before the shot, and the shooter's own streak entering it. Free throws are excluded.

Two limits of the source matter for anything downstream. Assists are only credited on made shots, so assisted can describe makes but never predict them. And a distance of 0 on a three-pointer means missing, not a heave from under the rim.

2 · The trapComparing to the average finds a cold hand that isn't there

The natural test is "hit rate after three straight makes versus the player's overall rate." That test is biased, and the bias points the wrong way. Take a fair coin flipped three times, and ask what fraction of flips that follow a heads are also heads:

sequenceflips after an Hfraction heads
T T Tnone–
T T Hnone–
T H TT0
H T TT0
T H HH1
H T HT0
H H TH, T½
H H HH, H1

Averaging the six defined fractions gives 5/12, about 42%, for a coin that is exactly 50%. Pooling all eight counted flips gives 4 of 8, exactly 50%. Both are correct arithmetic. The difference is that a real player is one sequence, so what you observe is one fraction, and its expected value is the biased 42%, not the pooled 50%.

Each make you spend defining "after k makes" is a make no longer available for the next shot. In short sequences with a fixed number of makes, that pulls the conditional rate down. It shrinks with sequence length and grows with k.

A 45% shooter with no hot hand, 20 shots per game, 100,000 simulated games

Per-game hit rate on shots following k straight makes, averaged across games. Nothing here is a hot or cold hand. It is pure selection.

measured rate after k makestrue make probability, 45%

This is what Gilovich, Vallone and Tversky's 1985 study measured, on 100-shot controlled sequences. Their "no difference" was in fact several points above what a coin would show. Miller and Sanjurjo's 2018 correction found a hot hand of roughly 11 to 13 points in that same data, in a no-defense setting.

3 · The fixShuffle the player's own shots and see what chance produces

Instead of comparing to the average, compare to what a no-hot-hand version of the same player would produce. Write each of the player's shot outcomes on a card, shuffle the cards within each game, deal them back out, and recompute the statistic. Repeat thousands of times. The pile of results is the null distribution. It carries the same finite-sequence bias as the real data, because it has the same number of makes in the same number of games. Only the order is gone.

The null mean is 48%, six points under his 54.5% average, from nothing but shuffling. That gap is the bias from section 2, computed for his exact numbers. Measured against it, his 78% sits in the top 4.5% of shuffles. One game is far too little to conclude anything, but the mechanics are the same at any scale.

4 · Four seasons of JokićNo hot hand, and the benchmark flips direction

The same test on all of his shots from 2022-23 through 2025-26, conditioning on three straight makes and three straight misses, with 5,000 within-game shuffles.

Null distributions, 5,000 within-game shuffles of Jokić's 5,976 shots

Top: hit rate on the 931 shots following 3 straight makes. Bottom: hit rate on the 411 shots following 3 straight misses. Same x-axis.

shuffled outcomesobserved rateoverall FG%, 57.6%

After three makes he shoots 57.3%. After three misses he shoots 57.2%. Both are his average, and the null is now tight enough, about 1.3 points wide, to call this a precise null result rather than a noisy one.

The null mean after 3 makes is 59.1%, above his 57.6% average, the opposite direction from the single game. Pooling across 335 games adds a second effect: games where he made a lot of shots produce many more "after 3 makes" opportunities, so high-percentage games dominate the pooled numerator. Within-game bias pulls down, between-game selection pulls up, and over a career the second wins. The raw average is the wrong benchmark in both directions. Only the shuffle gets it right for the actual data structure.

5 · The leagueAfter three makes, players shoot worse than chance predicts

The same test for every player with at least 1,000 attempts across the four seasons: 305 players, 2,000 shuffles each. For each, the statistic is the makes-minus-misses gap, expressed as a z-score against that player's own null.

Where 305 players land against their own null

z-score of (rate after 3 makes − rate after 3 misses) relative to the shuffled distribution. If no player had any streak effect, the bars would follow the dashed bell curve.

playersexpected if no streak effects, N(0, 1)

The distribution is shifted left. Twenty-nine players are significantly colder after three makes than their shuffles, against about 15 expected by chance. Only eight are significantly hotter, fewer than chance. Across all 305 the post-streak rate averages 1.3 points below the null.

This matches what tracking-data studies found: in games, the raw make rate drops after a streak because players take harder shots and defenses adjust. Whatever underlying hot hand exists is more than cancelled by shot selection.

Maxey is the standout

His post-streak rate is eight points below what shuffling his own games produces, over four standard deviations out. None of 2,000 shuffles got there. Lillard, Leonard and Zubac show the same pattern at smaller magnitude.

Reaves is the best hot-hand candidate, and it is modest

Three points of excess on 331 qualifying shots, p = 0.02. But with 305 players tested, eight hits below p = 0.05 is fewer than the fifteen noise alone would produce, so no individual hot hand survives a multiple-comparison correction yet.

6 · What this points toThe question is now shot selection, not shot making

The league-wide cold effect is consistent with players changing what they shoot after makes rather than losing the ability to make it. The shot table already carries the columns to separate the two:

  • Shot mix after a streak. For Maxey, compare share of threes, mean distance, and assisted share on shots after 3 makes against his baseline. If the mix shifts and the per-type rates hold, it is a selection story.
  • Twos versus threes. Condition on three made twos versus three made threes separately and check whether the next shot's value changes.
  • Game state. Split by score differential and time remaining, and flag heaves over 35 feet or inside the final three seconds of a period.
  • Attempt props, not make props. If streaks predict volume or shot type rather than accuracy, the tradeable signal lives in attempts and points lines, live in-game.
